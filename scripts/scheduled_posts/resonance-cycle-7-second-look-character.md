Cosmic scale can make a fantasy story feel strangely small if every person becomes an abstraction. *Ignite the Dark*, the seventh *Resonance Cycle* novel by Aaron Renfroe, faces that risk as Ty's world opens toward worldships, unfamiliar powers, and threats extending far beyond his original journey.

The useful anchor is loyalty. Offers of help matter differently when accepting them would require surrendering something that made the journey worth taking.

## Assistance can be another form of control

The encounters with more powerful institutions invite a question that has followed Ty since the beginning: what does a benefactor expect in return? A rescue or alliance can carry conditions that turn apparent security into another kind of dependence.

That gives the expanded setting an emotional purpose. The universe is not only larger; it contains more sophisticated ways to make an individual feel that his attachments are obstacles to sensible behaviour.

## Keeping people visible

The opening divine developments and the broadening cast demand attention. Their emotional effectiveness depends on keeping relationships understandable while the metaphysical vocabulary grows.

For some readers, the move toward science fantasy will be the appeal. For others, it will put distance between the series and the grounded preparation that first made Ty interesting. Both responses are reasonable. A worldship is not automatically more affecting than a conversation at home.

The strongest reason to read on is to see whether Ty can preserve personal commitments inside a setting that keeps encouraging a grander perspective. The reservation is that grandeur can become its own distraction. This volume is most interesting where it refuses to treat caring about particular people as an immature stage the hero should eventually outgrow.
