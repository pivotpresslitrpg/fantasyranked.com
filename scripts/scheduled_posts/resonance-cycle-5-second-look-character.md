A tournament is a public story about who deserves to be admired. *Torn Shroud*, Aaron Renfroe's fifth *Resonance Cycle* book, is more interesting when that public story rubs against private obligations. Ty does not arrive free of responsibilities, and the people around the competition are not simply spectators waiting to recognise the right hero.

The damaged refuge and the pressure on more than one world keep celebration from becoming entirely innocent. There are things that a successful appearance cannot put right.

## People inside the political spectacle

Signy and the dragon-related politics give the event relationships that extend beyond winning. That is the attraction for a fantasy reader less interested in a bracket than in what a contest reveals about a society.

The question is whose interests are served by the rules, the alliances, and the performance of power. A protagonist can become more visible without becoming more free.

## The awkward approach to the main event

The early dungeon and preparation material takes time. Readers carrying the emotional intensity of the previous book may be impatient for the next decisive development. Others will welcome the practical work that makes later choices feel connected to earlier effort.

That tension is worth admitting rather than disguising with a promise of nonstop tournament action. The volume has several jobs in the larger series, and their demands do not always point in the same direction.

Choose this instalment for the entanglement of spectacle and loyalty. Its potential lies in showing how a public contest can expose pressures that already exist elsewhere. The personal stakes are strongest when the characters remain people with obligations, not simply representatives of a faction or pieces moving toward the saga's next revelation.
