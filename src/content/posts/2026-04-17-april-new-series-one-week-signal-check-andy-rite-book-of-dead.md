Seven days after the April 7 launch window, the platform data from LitRPGTools.com and Harem-Lit.com is producing its first meaningful signal. Here's the one-week read on all three April series plus Book of the Dead 5's pre-release movement.

---

## Andy in the Apocalypse — Week One

**Signal direction**: Strong. Exceeding baseline expectations.

The core thesis held: Plum Parrot's existing Victor of Tucson readership transferred with low friction. The pattern we expected — Victor of Tucson readers finishing their re-read and immediately adding Andy in the Apocalypse — is exactly what the tracking data shows. Cross-catalog addition rates in the first week are comparable to a second-book launch in an established series, not a standalone new entry. That's a meaningful signal. First books in new series don't usually look like this.

The tone framing appears to be working. "Carve hope out of chaos" is not accidentally similar to Victor of Tucson's emotional register. Plum Parrot is writing the same kind of book — found family, warm character work, high-stakes setting — in a new context. Readers who respond to that formula are showing up.

What we're watching in week two: do the readers who add the book also *complete* it? Early add rates are necessary but not sufficient — conversion from add to read-through is where series health is confirmed. If read-through rates hold above the 65% benchmark we use as a signal threshold, Andy in the Apocalypse moves from "promising" to "established" on the Fantasy Ranked tracking list.

**Updated entry position**: Watch list → **Active monitoring, elevated priority.**

---

## Rite of Ascension — Week One

**Signal direction**: Modest. Consistent with debut author baseline.

Rite of Ascension by Marc Mulero is doing what a debut book by an unknown author should do in week one: quietly accumulating its first reader adds without breaking through into wider discovery. This isn't a failure signal — it's the normal trajectory for a series that doesn't have author-reputation transfer to lean on.

The premise elements are doing work. Readers who find the series through category browsing on both platforms are engaging with the dragon companion angle specifically — it's the described element that most consistently generates early adds. The weak-to-strong progression and arena battle framing are less distinctive (common genre elements), but the dragon companion as a relationship-driven narrative object is generating visible interest.

The limiting factor at one week: no community ratings yet. With no prior readership base, the first ratings matter disproportionately. If the first five LitRPGTools.com ratings average above 4.0, the series will generate discovery momentum from the platform's curation algorithm. If they cluster below 3.5, recovery is structurally difficult in the first 90 days.

**Verdict at one week**: Monitoring. Escalate to active tracking at first rating data — likely by April 22.

---

## Labyrinth of the Mad God — Week One

**Signal direction**: Minimal. As expected for gritty/slowburn positioning in a crowded launch window.

Labyrinth of the Mad God is the weakest signal of the three April 7 launches. The gritty/slowburn framing is a harder sell in a discovery environment dominated by faster-paced, more kinetic premises. The multi-dimensional twist — the premise element designed to differentiate it — requires explanation rather than communicating instantly, which is a friction point for catalog browsers.

This doesn't rule it out as a long-term performer. Slowburn series sometimes find their audience through word-of-mouth rather than algorithmic discovery. But at one week, it's not generating the kind of initial momentum that suggests breakout potential in this launch window.

**Verdict**: Low priority. Re-evaluate at 60 days.

---

## Book of the Dead 5 — Pre-Release Tracking (Release: April 22)

**Signal direction**: Dominant. Highest April pre-release signal in catalog.

Five days out from release, Book of the Dead 5 is doing exactly what a well-anticipated continuation release should do: back-catalog acceleration is running at its peak. New-to-series readers who discovered Book of the Dead through RinoZ's Chrysalis community are now working through Books 1-4 with compressed urgency. The "I need to finish before April 22" behavior is visible in the platform data.

The rating floor context matters here: Books 1-4 have maintained unusually consistent community ratings across both platforms. There's no mid-series score decay — the pattern that usually starts around Book 3 for long-running series. That consistency suggests RinoZ is delivering quality across the back catalog in a way that holds reader trust. Book 5 inherits that trust floor.

**Projection**: Book of the Dead 5 will be the highest-rated new release event on both platforms in April 2026. The signal confidence is high. This is a clean continuation of a well-rated series with an active readership prepping for release.
