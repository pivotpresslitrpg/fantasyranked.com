"Get paid to read books" is one of those search queries that attracts equal parts real opportunity and absolute nonsense. So let's do what we do here: rank every legitimate option, state the criteria, and call out the traps.

**Criteria:** actual pay, honesty of the arrangement, and how fast a skilled genre reader can realistically start. This list is written for readers of LitRPG, progression fantasy, and [power fantasy](/rankings/top-power-fantasy-books/) — the shelf where genre fluency is worth the most.

## 1. Professional Beta Reading — the Real Job

Beta reading tops this list because it's the one option where deep genre knowledge converts directly into professional rates. A beta reader tests a finished, unpublished draft and delivers structured feedback: pacing, continuity, character — and in LitRPG, the system itself. Does the progression math hold? Do the stat screens contradict each other? Is the power curve earned?

The cleanest on-ramp in our genres is [LitRPGTools.com](https://litrpgtools.com), which runs a vetted [Industry Professionals directory](https://litrpgtools.com/industry-professionals) where beta readers list services, set their own rates, and get contacted by authors directly. Two facts put this at #1:

- **The pay is real.** The platform's own applicant guidance cites the industry norm at roughly **$250–500 USD per 50k words** for a full pass, rush work at 1.5–2×. LitRPG novels run long; do the math on a 150k-word draft.
- **The gate is honest.** Applicants must confirm they've read **at least 25 books** in LitRPG or progression fantasy. Your qualification is the reading you've already done.

Approved readers also get matched to private paid **casting calls** — briefs posted by authors and publishers looking for exactly your profile. Applications are free at [litrpgtools.com/join](https://litrpgtools.com/join), reviewed in about 5–7 days.

## 2. Alpha Reading — the Premium Add-On

Alpha reading is a faster, story-first pass on early drafts, aimed at structural problems while they're still cheap to fix. It ranks second not because it pays less per hour but because it isn't usually a standalone gig — on LitRPGTools it's an opt-in service you attach to a beta reader profile. For readers who can turn a manuscript around quickly and think in story structure, it's the natural upsell. Offer both.

## 3. Editing-Adjacent Services — the Ladder Up

Proofreading and copyediting pay more per project than beta reading, but they're different skills with a real learning curve — style guides, error taxonomies, consistency tracking — and the market expects demonstrated competence, not enthusiasm. Worth naming because beta reading is the traditional first rung of exactly this ladder: betas who consistently catch continuity and clarity issues often grow into paid editorial work. Start at #1, climb deliberately.

## 4. ARC and Early Reader Programs — Paid in Books

Advance Reader Copies are the classic arrangement: free early books in exchange for honest reviews at launch. For a reader burning through [new releases](/rankings/new-litrpg-releases/) anyway, it's genuinely good value — but let's be precise: **this is compensation in books, not money.** It costs you nothing, pays you nothing, and keeps your TBR stocked. Rank it accordingly.

## 5. Book Reviewing — Do It for Love, Not Money

Last place, with a warning label. Genuine reader reviews are the lifeblood of the genre — and they're unpaid by design. Anyone offering to *pay you* for retail reviews is asking you to violate retailer policies and mislead other readers; that's not a side hustle, it's a scam with your name on it. Review because you have opinions (we sympathize). If you want money for your reading skill, that's what #1 is for.

## The Bottom Line

Ranked honestly: professional beta reading is the only entry on this list that turns genre expertise into professional rates, alpha reading is its premium add-on, editing is the long-term ladder, ARCs pay in books, and reviewing pays in satisfaction.
