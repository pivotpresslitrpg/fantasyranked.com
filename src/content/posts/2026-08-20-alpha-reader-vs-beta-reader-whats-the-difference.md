Alpha reader versus beta reader is the most commonly blurred distinction in indie publishing, so let's fix it in two sentences. An **alpha reader** critiques an early, sometimes unfinished draft — fast, story-first, hunting structural problems while they're still cheap to fix. A **beta reader** tests the *finished* draft — a detailed pass over pacing, continuity, character, and (in our genres) whether the system math actually holds.

Same instinct, different phase of the pipeline, different skills, different pay. Here's the full breakdown — including how readers get paid to do both.

## What Does an Alpha Reader Do?

An alpha reader is the first outside brain on a manuscript. The draft may be rough, missing scenes, or actively being written. The alpha's job is to react at the story level, quickly:

- Is the premise delivering, or did the book drift from its own promise?
- Does the structure work — arcs, act turns, the shape of the power curve?
- Should this subplot die now, before the author builds half a book on top of it?

Speed matters more than polish. An alpha who returns big-picture reactions in days is worth more than one who returns line notes in months, because the entire value of alpha feedback is that it arrives *while the draft can still change cheaply*.

## What Does a Beta Reader Do?

A beta reader tests a complete draft the way a QA tester runs a finished build. The feedback is beat-level and specific: where the pacing sagged, which chapter confused them, which character promise never paid off.

In LitRPG and [power fantasy](/rankings/top-power-fantasy-books/), the beta job description grows teeth. A genre-fluent beta is expected to catch:

1. Progression math that doesn't add up between chapters
2. Stat blocks and skills that contradict themselves across a long series
3. Power curves that spike or stall in ways that kill tension
4. Mechanics that read as recycled from whatever topped the charts last year

That's specialist work, and the market has finally started pricing it like specialist work.

## Alpha vs Beta Reader: The Head-to-Head

- **Timing:** Alpha reads an early draft; beta reads the finished draft. Alpha first, always.
- **Depth:** Alpha is broad and structural; beta is detailed and beat-level.
- **Speed:** Alpha turnaround is fast by design; beta passes take longer because they cover everything.
- **Deliverable:** Alpha delivers big-picture reactions; beta delivers a structured report or inline notes.
- **Skill floor:** Both demand genre fluency, but beta reading demands the stamina to document 200,000 words of findings, not just react to them.

Ranking them is a category error — they're sequential, not competitive. But if you're a reader deciding which service to *offer*: beta reading is the anchor skill, and alpha reading is the premium add-on for people who can turn work around fast.
