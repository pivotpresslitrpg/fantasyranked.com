## What Is Dark Fantasy LitRPG?

Dark fantasy LitRPG is a subgenre where system-driven progression mechanics operate inside morally brutal, high-stakes worlds. It is characterized by protagonist builds with lethal specialization, system sheets that carry narrative weight, and a tone that refuses to soften the consequences of power.

*Shadow in Madness* — the second entry in Aaron Renfroe's **Spite the Dark** series — lands squarely in that category and then pushes further. This is not a book about leveling up toward comfort. It is a book about what it costs to be the weapon someone else needs you to be.

---

## What Is Shadow in Madness About?

*Shadow in Madness* picks up with **Kaden Yamaguchi**, a level 6 Steel Shadow — a class that reads less like a hero's archetype and more like a precision instrument. He is an assassin with a SPITE unit (a bonded alien armor system with its own AI and upgrade tree), a growing set of Catalyst powers, and exactly one active summon: **Paisley**, a Ziltrax Locksmith who manifests as a three-foot winged millipede with a human woman embedded in its torso.

That last detail is not incidental. It tells you everything about what Renfroe is doing here.

The opening chapter drops Kaden into a stakeout on a Washington, D.C. senator connected to a faction called Gentex and a separate group of entities called Deltas — neither of which he fully understands, and both of which are actively dangerous. The political web he's navigating involves Chronarchs, time-sensitive faction maneuvering, and the ever-present threat of the Madness, a parallel reality that functions as both origin plane for summoned creatures and existential hazard. He is not the most powerful person in the room. He is the most prepared.

## How Does the Progression System Work in Spite the Dark?

The system in *Spite the Dark* runs on two parallel tracks — and this is where Renfroe's design genuinely separates itself from the crowded field.

Kaden has a **Catalyst HUD**, which governs class abilities, summons, and skill enhancements. He also has a **SPITE HUD**, which tracks the alien armor's independent evolution. These two systems interact but do not duplicate each other. The Catalyst gives him powers like *Heartfinder*, *Shadow Meld*, and *Enter Madness*. The SPITE unit gives him the *Momentum Array*, *Ablative Coating*, and the *Atticus* — a modular blade that can extend, whip, and chain depending on intent and skill investment.

The dual-track design produces real strategic texture. When Kaden needs to breach a window fifteen feet up a glazed brick wall, the solution isn't a single stat check — it's a live coordination between the Momentum Array, the Atticus used as a piton, Paisley's lockpicking, and Samantha's tactical overlay. That kind of layered problem-solving is what separates strong LitRPG from systems that just award numbers without meaning.

Readers who gravitate toward the mechanical depth of *Dungeon Crawler Carl* by Matt Dinniman or the tight build optimization in *He Who Fights With Monsters* by Jason Cheyne will find the SPITE/Catalyst split immediately legible — and arguably more structurally interesting because neither system alone is sufficient.

## Is the Summoner Class Done Well Here?

Summoner builds are notoriously difficult to execute in LitRPG. They tend to slide into either pure utility (the summon does everything, the protagonist watches) or pure flavor (the summon exists but never meaningfully changes the calculus of a fight). Renfroe avoids both traps.

Paisley costs 6 HP to summon, has a 30-minute timer extendable only by feeding her stickers (yes, physical stickers), is capped at six unlocks per summoning per Kaden's level, and can be disrupted. She has personality — her voice is compared to Tinkerbell, she expresses opinions on confined spaces, and she cheerfully identifies a killing floor's corpse as a victim of a "Face Stealing" Catalyst with the detached expertise of something that has been alive since before the planet cooled.

She is also genuinely useful in ways that reshape the mission rather than just executing it. That balance — a summon who is powerful, limited, and characterful — is harder to pull off than it looks. It earns the build its place on the page.

## How Does Shadow in Madness Compare to Other LitRPG Series?

For readers building out a [best isekai novels](/rankings/best-isekai-novels/) reading list or looking for [new LitRPG releases](/rankings/new-litrpg-releases/) that go somewhere darker than the genre average, *Spite the Dark* belongs in the conversation alongside titles like *The Land* by Aleron Kong for its world-depth ambition, and *Primal Hunter* by Zogarth for its willingness to let a protagonist operate in morally grey territory without softening the edges.

The tone is closer to *The Completionist Chronicles* in its earnestness about system rules, but the content is darker — the opening chapter involves a senator's killing floor, multiple body bags, and a locked attic lined in plastic. Renfroe is not writing comfort fantasy. He is writing about a man who has accepted that he is a tool, and is trying to remain a person anyway.

That psychological layer — the friction between Kaden's clinical assassin competence and his still-functional emotional life, the offhand warmth between him and Vasilis, the sardonic rapport with Samantha — is what keeps *Shadow in Madness* from becoming a pure action delivery vehicle.

## Where It Ranks

**Verdict: Solid mid-to-upper tier in the dark isekai LitRPG space — and the summoner build alone justifies the read.**

*Shadow in Madness* is not trying to be the most accessible book on the [top power fantasy books](/rankings/top-power-fantasy-books/) list. It's designed for readers who want dual-track progression, a tactically competent protagonist who isn't overpowered by page one, and a world complex enough to require actual attention. The Paisley summoning sequence in the opening chapter is one of the more carefully constructed uses of a summon mechanic in recent LitRPG — specific, limited, characterful, and plot-functional all at once.

Aaron Renfroe is building something with genuine structural ambition here. *Spite the Dark* rewards readers who show up ready to track faction politics alongside skill trees. If that sounds like your kind of power fantasy, this series earns a slot on your list.
