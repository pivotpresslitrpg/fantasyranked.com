We rank books for a living, so this month we're ranking something different: platform upgrades. [LitRPGTools.com](https://litrpgtools.com) — the community database that powers much of the ranking data we lean on here — shipped five significant discovery upgrades in the first half of July 2026. We ranked them by one criterion: **how much does this change how you'll actually find your next book?**

Spoiler: the top spot wasn't close.

## 1. Narrator-First Discovery

The winner by a mile. Power fantasy is an audio-heavy genre — for a big slice of readers, "what should I read next?" really means "what should I listen to next?" — and until now, no database treated the narrator as a first-class answer to that question.

Now: every search result on LitRPGTools.com carries a **"Narrated by" credit**, narrators have **public profiles with market stats**, a **Top Narrated rail** on the homepage shows the genre's most-listened voices, and you can **follow a narrator** for alerts the day their next book drops. Discovering by voice is a genuinely new axis for the genre — if a favorite narrator has carried you through one series, their catalog is now a ranked reading list waiting to happen. (Narrator-credit tooling is rolling out across the site's sister platform too, so expect this axis to keep deepening.)

## 2. The Upcoming Releases Radar

The homepage now runs an **Upcoming Releases rail fed by real preorder data**. That sounds small; it isn't. Release-radar duty for this genre has historically meant stitching together Discord rumors, newsletter fragments, and storefront searching. A single preorder-driven calendar for LitRPG and progression fantasy replaces all of it — and it makes our own [new LitRPG releases ranking](/rankings/new-litrpg-releases/) meaningfully easier to compile. Freshness is a ranking signal; now readers get it at the source.

## 3. The Human-Narration-Only Policy

LitRPGTools.com **banned AI "Virtual Voice" narration from its catalog** — it can't be ingested, displayed, or recommended. Ranked third because most readers won't see it directly, but it protects the integrity of everything ranked above: a Top Narrated rail only means something if every voice on it is an actual performance. As storefronts flood with synthetic audio, the genre's database of record drawing this line is quiet infrastructure for every audio ranking that comes after.

## 4. The Author Interviews Hub

A new public **interviews hub**, spotlighted on the homepage and linked from author profiles. Context is underrated discovery: knowing why an author built their power system the way they did is often what converts a "maybe" into a one-click. It also gives the genre a real editorial record — the kind of thing you wish existed when you're trying to rank a ten-year-old series' influence.

## 5. Clean Author Handles & Public Professional Pages

Verified authors now get **readable public profile URLs**, and the platform launched **public pages for industry professionals** — narrators, cover artists, publishers — with a proper application pipeline behind them. Fifth place only because the payoff is longer-term: this is plumbing. But it's plumbing that makes every person behind a book findable and linkable, and databases win on exactly this kind of unglamorous completeness.

## The Takeaway

Five upgrades, one theme: [LitRPGTools.com](https://litrpgtools.com) is betting that discovery — not just data — is what a genre platform is for, and this month's shipping pace backs it up. Our [top power fantasy rankings](/rankings/top-power-fantasy-books/) draw on community data from exactly these systems, so when the platform's discovery gets sharper, so do the rankings.

We'll rank next month's wave when it lands. Based on this month's pace, there will be one.
