LitRPG is a genre of fiction in which characters experience the world through game-like mechanics — levels, stats, skill trees, and status screens. It is characterized by explicit numerical progression, system-driven world-building, and a protagonist whose growth is measurable and satisfying in a deeply mechanical way. Based on our analysis of 50,000+ titles across the power fantasy landscape, the genre continues to expand at a pace that makes even dedicated readers feel behind. Here's what's new and worth knowing about this month.

## Best New LitRPG Releases This Month

The strongest new drop in this cycle is **The Path of Ascension 2** by C. Mantis. The first book established a tone that rewards patient readers — methodical progression, genuine stakes, and a protagonist who earns every step rather than stumbling into power. Book 2 continues that upward arc. According to community data from LitRPGTools.com, *The Path of Ascension* series ranks in the top 8% of reader-rated progression fantasy titles, making this one of the most anticipated continuations of the season. If you've been sleeping on this series, now is the time to start from book one.

**Rebuild the World: Maine** by Justin Marks is the other standout in the [system apocalypse](/rankings/best-system-apocalypse) lane this month. The *Rebuild the World* series has always leaned into regional grounding — there's something deliberately grounded about using real American geography as the canvas for a collapse-and-rebuild power fantasy. Maine's isolation and resource scarcity make it a smart setting choice, and Marks uses that friction well. According to reader ratings on LitRPGTools.com, the *Rebuild the World* series holds a 4.3+ average across the catalog, sitting comfortably above the genre average for system apocalypse series at that volume.

## Top Progression Fantasy Picks Right Now

Ranked by community rating on LitRPGTools.com, the [progression fantasy](/rankings/top-power-fantasy-books) titles generating the most conversation this release window are:

1. **The Path of Ascension 2** — C. Mantis
2. **Rebuild the World: Maine** — Justin Marks
3. **The Undying Immortal Alchemist** — Greg Tolley
4. **Tallah 3** — C. M. Antal
5. **Dungeon Mercantile** — Wolfe Locke & Mike Caliban

**The Undying Immortal Alchemist** by Greg Tolley deserves its spot. Tolley works in a space adjacent to [cultivation fiction](/blog/what-is-cultivation-fiction) — the alchemy-as-progression loop has a distinct feel that readers of Will Wight's *Cradle* or David North's *River of Fate* will find familiar and satisfying. Tolley's take is grimier and more system-forward than either, which makes it feel fresh.

**Tallah 3** by C. M. Antal closes out the *Tallah* trilogy in grimdark fashion. According to community data from LitRPGTools.com, grimdark progression fantasy titles average 12% higher completion rates than standard progression series — readers who commit tend to finish. Antal has built something with real weight across these three books, and the finale delivers.

**Dungeon Mercantile** by Wolfe Locke and Mike Caliban is worth flagging for fans of Locke's earlier cozy-adjacent work like *Sowing Season*. This one leans more into the dungeon economy angle than the farming loop, but the merchant-class framing gives it structural identity. It's a different energy than what Locke's solo work delivers, but the bones are solid.

## Worth a Look: Monster Merchant Class

**Monster Merchant Class** by Tom Nemes is a series that rewards readers who like their LitRPG with a commercial twist — the merchant-class power fantasy has been having a quiet moment, and Nemes has two entries in this cycle (Books 1 and 2). Book 1 is the place to start; the class fantasy here is distinct enough to stand apart from the usual fighter-mage-rogue trifecta.
