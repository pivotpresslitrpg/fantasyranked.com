LitRPG is a genre defined by characters who grow in power through explicit game-like systems — leveling, stats, skills, and class progression all tracked on the page. It is characterized by satisfying power curves, strategic combat, and worlds where the rules of progression matter as much as the story itself.

If *Sowing Season* hit the right notes for you — that blend of measured power growth, world-building with real stakes, and characters who earn every rank — you're in the right place. Below are eight picks from across the LitRPG and progression fantasy landscape that deliver the same core satisfaction.

## What to Read After Sowing Season

These recommendations are ordered by how closely they mirror the tonal and mechanical DNA of *Sowing Season*. Each one rewards readers who want meaningful progression, not just power spikes.

### 1. Dungeon Crawler Carl by Matt Dinniman

The apocalypse arrives without warning, and suddenly Earth's survivors are trapped inside a televised alien dungeon. Carl and his ex-girlfriend's prize-winning show cat, Princess Donut, have to fight their way through increasingly lethal floors while building a fanbase the dungeon masters can't ignore. This is LitRPG at its most propulsive — sharp, funny, and relentlessly inventive. If you want stakes and momentum, start here and don't stop.

### 2. Carl's Doomsday Scenario (Dungeon Crawler Carl #2) by Matt Dinniman

The training floors are over. *Carl's Doomsday Scenario* is where the real games begin, and the difficulty curve matches the ambition. A *New York Times* bestseller, this second entry deepens both the dungeon's mechanics and the emotional weight of Carl and Princess Donut's fight to survive. Once you're invested in the first book, this one is mandatory.

### 3. Mark of the Fool by J.M. Clarke

The gods chose Alex Roth as a Hero. He said no. Rather than serve the prophecy, he enrolls in the world's greatest wizardry academy and starts building his own path — using the Fool's mark in ways nobody anticipated. This is progression fantasy with genuine academic structure and a protagonist who outsmarts destiny through preparation and cleverness. If *Sowing Season*'s methodical growth appealed to you, *Mark of the Fool* is a natural next read.

### 4. Fire and Song (Warformed: Stormweaver #2) by Bryce O'Connor

Reidon Ward has gone from the weakest cadet at the Galens Institute to one of the most feared, and his Device — Shido — is something nobody has seen before. *Fire and Song* picks up as the Sectionals tournament arrives and the real competition begins. Pure progression fantasy with tournament structure and a protagonist who earns every step of his ascent.

### 5. Restarting the Apocalypse by Michael Chatfield

Two combat veterans watched Harmonia fall. Now they've been sent back 130 years before the collapse — no stats, no skills, no safety net. Just hard-won tactical knowledge and the weight of knowing exactly what's coming. This is system apocalypse done with unusual emotional depth, and the fifth entry in a series that clearly knows how to sustain momentum.

### 6. Apocalypse BREAKER 2 by Aaron Renfroe & Sean Oswald

A demonic intelligence wants Dean's body. A crusade is mobilizing to destroy Earth before it survives System integration. And the System itself is using him as a pawn. *Apocalypse BREAKER 2* is high-octane system apocalypse with genuine cosmic stakes — the kind of book where the power fantasy serves a story that actually matters.

### 7. The Janitor Killed the World Boss by Aaron Renfroe

The title earns its premise. This LitRPG debut leans into the underdog formula with confidence, building toward a payoff that justifies the journey. Fans of protagonists who aren't supposed to win but do anyway will find a lot to love here.

### 8. Battle Mage Farmer, Book 1: Domestication by Seth Ring

Retired war mage John Sutton wants a quiet farm. What he gets is a slow-building apocalypse tracked by a Doom Points counter that ticks toward 100. The blend of pastoral slice-of-life and looming magical warfare is a genuine tonal achievement — and it scratches an itch that pure combat-focused LitRPG often misses.
