Rank the skills that sell a power fantasy novel and cover art sits uncomfortably near the top — above the blurb, above the title, arguably above the first chapter, because none of those get seen if the thumbnail loses. A cover in this genre has to broadcast subgenre, tone, and power fantasy in under a second at 60 pixels wide. That is a rare, specific, commercial skill.

This post is for the artists who have it and want to know where the paying work lives. We'll rank the venues while we're at it, because that's what we do here.

## Where Is the Demand for Fantasy Cover Art?

Concentrated exactly where the books are: in series-driven indie genres. LitRPG, progression fantasy, and [power fantasy](/rankings/top-power-fantasy-books/) run on long series with fast release schedules — and every installment needs a cover that matches its siblings. That's the economics artists should care about: a single author relationship in a series genre isn't one commission, it's a pipeline. Covers, character art, world maps, audiobook variants, hardback interiors, ad creative.

The problem was never demand. It was that the demand had no address — authors hunted through social feeds and Discords, artists shouted portfolios into the void, and the match rate was terrible in both directions.
