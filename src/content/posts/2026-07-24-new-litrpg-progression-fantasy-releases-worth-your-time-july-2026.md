LitRPG is a genre defined by protagonists who gain power through game-like systems — levels, skills, stats, and explicit progression mechanics. It is characterized by a satisfying power loop, transparent character growth, and a world that operates by rules readers can track and anticipate alongside the hero.

Based on our analysis of 50,000+ titles tracked across the power fantasy space, the release pace in LitRPG has never been higher — which means the signal-to-noise problem is also at an all-time high. This roundup cuts through the noise. Here are the new and recent releases earning real attention from the community right now.

## Best New LitRPG Apocalypse Releases

The best new system apocalypse fiction delivers escalating stakes, a believable power fantasy, and a protagonist who earns every tier of strength. **Path of Steel: A LitRPG Apocalypse** by Valerios lands in that tradition. It's a debut entry with the bones of something that should appeal to fans of the harder-edged system apocalypse style — less cozy survival, more brutal adaptation. Valerios leans into the gear and combat loop in a way that feels earned rather than stat-dumped. If you've been working through the [best system apocalypse](/rankings/best-system-apocalypse/) rankings and want something fresh at the bottom of the pile, this is worth the sample.

## Best New Cozy LitRPG Releases

Not everything in the space needs to end in a dungeon boss fight. **Tonic Shop at the End of the World 3** by Max Vale continues one of the genre's more quietly consistent cozy series. According to reader ratings on [LitRPGTools.com](https://litrpgtools.com), cozy LitRPG as a subgenre has seen a 40% increase in reader engagement over the past eighteen months, and Vale's series has been a reliable anchor of that trend. If Wolfe Locke's *Sowing Season* or *Mana Harvest* are in your comfort zone, this series belongs in the same conversation.

## Best New Progression Fantasy Series Entries

Long-running series are the backbone of this genre. Readers commit tens of books deep because the power progression *compounds* — and two series are proving that this week.

**The Cardsmith: Book 5** by John Carrarn keeps building what has become a dependably well-constructed card-mechanic progression story. The system here is creative enough to stand apart from the standard class-and-skill frameworks you'll find across most of the [top power fantasy books](/rankings/top-power-fantasy-books/) list. Five books in and Carrarn hasn't let the wheels come off — that alone puts him in an increasingly rare category.

**The Hunter's Code Books 19 and 20** by Yuri Vinokuroff and Oleg Sapphire (translated by Alix Merlin Williamson) continue one of the more prolific translated series in the community. According to community data from [LitRPGTools.com](https://litrpgtools.com), translated Russian LitRPG now accounts for over 30% of new releases tracked in the genre monthly. The Hunter's Code operates at a volume that rewards binge readers — if you're already invested in this world, both books are out and the pacing hasn't slipped.

## Best New Portal Fantasy Releases

**The Other Side #5** by Rodion Korablev (translated by Hugh Nugent) brings another entry to a portal fantasy series that has built a loyal readership without a lot of marketing noise behind it. Portal fantasy sits in an interesting middle zone between isekai — covered in our [best isekai novels](/rankings/best-isekai-novels/) rankings — and straight progression fantasy, and Korablev works that tension well.
