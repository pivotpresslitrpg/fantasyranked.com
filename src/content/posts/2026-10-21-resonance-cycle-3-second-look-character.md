The past in *Past's Price* is not safely behind anyone. Aaron Renfroe's third *Resonance Cycle* novel makes history something Ty must encounter through the damaged remains of another world, while the people and preparations on Earth continue to give his journey urgency.

That is a compelling shift for portal fantasy. A strange destination becomes more than scenery or a source of resources when the story asks who lived there, what happened to them, and what their losses mean for the visitor.

## Ruins should belong to someone

The Ako material works as an invitation to question the usual adventurer's gaze. An unfamiliar place is not empty simply because the protagonist has only just arrived. It has a history that does not begin with his needs.

Ty's growing responsibilities make that distinction important. Protecting a future and understanding a past belong to the same moral problem: how much attention is owed to lives outside the hero's immediate circle?

## The risk of revelation overload

Historical revelations can deepen a world, but they can also become a stream of explanations that outpaces emotional response. The book's appeal depends partly on whether the reader enjoys assembling implications across earlier volumes.

This is not the instalment to choose for a lightly connected quest with a fresh cast. The existing relationships and accumulated uncertainties do substantial work. Nor is the change of setting simply a promise of relaxed exploration.

For readers invested in the series, the strongest reason to continue is the way discovery unsettles Ty's position. He is not only learning how to become stronger. He is learning that the world he wants to influence has already suffered through stories he was not there to witness.
