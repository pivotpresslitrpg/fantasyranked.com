Authors have always produced more than the books: the cut arc, the character sheet, the map draft, the narrator's blooper reel. The question is how that material reaches readers, and until recently there were two answers: pay for it on Patreon, or dig for it in a newsletter. [LitRPGTools.com](https://litrpgtools.com) has added a third with **Follower Bonus Content**, a free library unlocked by following the creator.

Three models, one job. Here's how they rank for the reader, with the criteria stated up front: what it costs, how much friction stands between you and the content, whether you can actually *find* anything, and how easily access gets lost.

## 1. Follower Bonus Content: free, findable, follow-gated

The new entry ranks first, and the reason is structural rather than novelty. Follower Bonus Content is a searchable library where the only entitlement is a current follow. No payment, no membership, no Patreon link; the platform forbids all of them as gates. Follow a creator and their full posts open; unfollow and they relock.

What that solves is discovery. Extras are filed by content type (bonus chapters, short stories, deleted scenes, behind-the-scenes, interviews, transcripts, essays, announcements) and by format, from text and galleries to page-ordered comics and audio, each attached to a specific book or series. You can browse the whole genre's extras in one place, sorted newest first, instead of knowing in advance which author has a Patreon and which has a Discord.

The honest costs: it's safe-for-work only, with zero tolerance and a ban for violations, so anything spicy stays elsewhere. It excludes full books and audiobooks, and creators are warned off reposting anything enrolled in KDP Select, so this is extras, never editions. And the library is young. The mechanism is live and well built; the volume depends on creators showing up.

**Cost:** nothing. **Friction:** one click. **Findability:** the best of the three by a wide margin. **Risk of loss:** unfollow and it's gone, which is fair.

## 2. Newsletter extras: free, but you'll never find it again

The newsletter bonus scene is the genre's old reliable. Free, direct, and delivered by an author who wants you to read it. It ranks second because of everything that happens after delivery.

Newsletter extras are unsearchable. The deleted scene you loved eighteen months ago lives in an email you deleted, or a PDF link that expired, or a "subscribers-only page" whose URL you never bookmarked. There's no library, no filter, no way to browse what an author has released across their career. Each author's extras are an island, and the map is your inbox.

**Cost:** an email address and the inbox noise that comes with it. **Friction:** low at signup, high on every retrieval. **Findability:** poor. **Risk of loss:** high. Unsubscribe, lose the email, or let the link expire.

## 3. Patreon tiers: the right tool for a different job

Patreon ranks third *for the reader looking for extras*, and it's worth being precise about why, because it wins on a different question entirely.

If what you want is to fund an author directly, to keep someone writing, Patreon is the best tool available, and nothing here replaces it. But as a distribution model for deleted scenes and art, it fragments the fandom. Every creator's extras sit behind a separate subscription with its own tier ladder, there's no cross-creator search, and the moment you stop paying, the archive goes dark. A reader who follows twenty authors cannot reasonably fund twenty tiers to read twenty deleted scenes.

**Cost:** monthly, per creator. **Friction:** a subscription per author. **Findability:** none across creators. **Risk of loss:** total, the month you cancel.

## The comparison in one view

Ranked for a reader who wants extras from the [power fantasy](/rankings/top-power-fantasy-books/) authors they follow:

1. **Follower Bonus Content.** Free, one action, genre-wide search, SFW-only, extras not editions.
2. **Newsletter extras.** Free, direct, unsearchable, easily lost.
3. **Patreon tiers.** Paid per creator, no cross-creator discovery, best for funding rather than finding.

None of these is going away, and they aren't really competitors. Patreon funds. Newsletters reach. A follow-to-read library *organizes* — and organization was the missing piece.

## The bottom line

The cheapest lock in publishing is a follow button, and putting the genre's extras behind it is the right call for readers. The library is at [litrpgtools.com/bonus-content](https://litrpgtools.com/bonus-content); the useful move is to follow the authors you already read and let the shelf fill.
