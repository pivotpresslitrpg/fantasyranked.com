[LitRPGTools.com](https://litrpgtools.com) has shipped **Author Studio**, a free in-browser composer for comics, Patreon headers, social graphics, and character art. The obvious question is whether a genre platform's design tool can stand next to a general one that authors already use. Ranked on five criteria that actually matter to a working fantasy author, the answer is: on the genre-specific work, it's ahead; on everything else, it's honest about not trying.

Criteria, stated up front: gets the sizes right, handles comics natively, keeps drafts private, lets you leave with your work, and what it costs.

## 1. Right sizes, without a fight

Author Studio wins this one outright, and it's the criterion authors lose the most time to.

It ships ten exact-size canvases. Four comic pages: 2:3 print, 4:5 social, square, webtoon. Six marketing canvases at destination sizes: social post 1080 by 1080 and 1080 by 1350, story or reel 1080 by 1920, link preview 1200 by 628, Patreon header 1600 by 400, Patreon tier art 620 by 300. You pick the destination and the proportions are correct before you place an image, and the export is the exact size the destination expects.

A general tool can produce any of those sizes, but you have to know them, set them, and check the export. The Studio's advantage isn't capability. It's that the right answer is the default.

## 2. Comic-native tools

Second clear win, because a general design app was never built for sequential art.

The Studio has panel templates and freeform pages, reframing inside a panel without altering the source image, panel splitting, frame shapes, and page ordering. Lettering is the real test: speech and caption balloons with draggable tails, edited in place by double-clicking, from a searchable typeface list. Add layers with visibility and locking, multi-select, groups, alignment, distribution, snapping to page edges and neighbors, and hard-edge image masks from seven presets or any closed shape you've drawn.

That's a comic editor. A general tool gives you a speech-bubble sticker and a grid. The gap is the work you'd otherwise do by hand.

Published comics also get a reader-side payoff a design app can't offer: a panel-by-panel reading mode on the platform that resumes at the exact panel across devices.

## 3. Private by default

Studio projects are owned, titled canvases with no public URL, no follower gate, and no review queue. They save to your account as you work; if another copy changed first, the editor stops and asks you to reload rather than overwriting. Publishing is a separate, deliberate move into the site's Bonus Content library.

A general tool is private too, so call this even on paper. In practice, the Studio's separation between "drafting" and "publishing" is cleaner, because publishing is a different product with its own review.

## 4. Leaving with your work

The Studio exports a full-resolution PNG of any page and a project file that reopens with the whole composition: panels, lettering, page order. Portable projects are what make a tool safe to invest evenings in.

General tools vary here, and the free tiers tend to keep the editable version inside their walls. Advantage Studio, with the caveat that the project format is the Studio's own.

## 5. Cost, and the credit line

On LitRPGTools, Author Studio is free. Every export carries a "Made on litrpgtools.com" credit in a band *below* the artwork, never across it; on exact-size marketing canvases the band is inset so the file still comes out at the advertised size. The platform's own reasoning is that a band under the page is a credit and a mark across the page is a watermark, and authors are right to resent the second. Uploaded art counts against the account's shared media allowance.

A free general tool usually gates the useful exports, sizes, or transparent backgrounds behind a subscription. A credit band is a smaller price than a subscription. Whether it's the right price is your call.

## Where the general tool still wins

Rank honestly or don't rank. A general design app beats the Studio on template breadth across every category, on collaboration and brand kits, on mobile editing, and on stock assets. The Studio is images-only, desktop-first for composition, and new, which means its template library will grow rather than arriving complete. If your graphics need is a wedding-invitation aesthetic, the Studio is the wrong tool.

## The ranking

For a fantasy author making comics, Patreon art, and social graphics from their own artwork:

1. **Right sizes:** Studio.
2. **Comic tools:** Studio, by a wide margin.
3. **Privacy:** even.
4. **Portability:** Studio.
5. **Cost:** Studio, at the price of a credit line.

Where the general tool wins, it wins on breadth, not on any of the five things above. Keep both. Do the genre work in the Studio and the rest wherever you already do it.
